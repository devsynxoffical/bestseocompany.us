The Website Redesign and Migration SEO Checklist

In short

A safe SEO migration starts with a full crawl and a URL map that sends every old page to its closest new page with a 301 redirect. Keep staging blocked from search engines, check canonicals and internal links, submit new sitemaps, and use Search Console's Change of Address tool when moving domains. Monitor traffic, errors and rankings for several months.

Key takeaways
  • Benchmark traffic, rankings and top pages before you change anything.
  • Every old URL with traffic or links needs a 301 redirect to its closest new match.
  • Block staging from search engines, then remove that block at launch.
  • Monitor Search Console, analytics and redirects closely for several months after launch.

Before you start: benchmark and crawl

Record where you stand before anything changes. Export organic traffic by landing page, top queries from Search Console and key conversion data. Note rankings for your most important keywords and save copies of current sitemaps.

Crawl the existing site with a tool such as Screaming Frog or Sitebulb. Combine that list with URLs from Search Console, analytics and backlink tools, since crawls miss orphaned pages. This full list becomes the base of your redirect map.

  • Export landing page traffic for at least the last 12 months.
  • List pages with external backlinks.
  • Save current titles, meta descriptions and headings.
  • Record current Core Web Vitals and index coverage.

Map every URL

Build a spreadsheet with each old URL and its new destination. Match pages one to one wherever possible, using the closest equivalent content. Redirecting everything to the home page wastes the value of old pages and confuses users.

Keep URLs the same where you can, since unchanged URLs need no redirect. If a page is being removed with no replacement, decide whether it should redirect to a close parent page or return a 404 or 410. Get sign-off on the map before development finishes.

Old URL situationAction
Same content, new URL301 to the new URL
Content merged into another page301 to the merged page
Page removed, close alternative exists301 to the closest alternative
Page removed, no alternativeReturn 404 or 410
URL unchangedNo redirect needed

Set up staging safely

Your staging site should never be indexed. Password protection is the most reliable option, and noindex or a robots.txt block can add another layer. Do not let staging URLs leak into sitemaps or internal links.

Use staging to check SEO elements before launch. Compare titles, meta descriptions, headings, structured data and body content against the old site. Make sure important content was not dropped during the redesign.

  • Protect staging with a password.
  • Check that canonical tags point to production URLs, not staging.
  • Confirm internal links use the new final URLs.
  • Test redirects on staging if your setup allows it.

Launch day checklist

Launch at a quiet time for your business, with developers available to fix issues. Remove any noindex tags and robots.txt blocks carried over from staging. This is one of the most common and damaging migration mistakes.

Test redirects from your map right away, using a crawler in list mode. Check for redirect chains and loops, and make sure redirects use 301 status codes. Submit the new XML sitemap in Search Console and confirm the robots.txt file is correct.

  • Remove staging noindex and robots.txt blocks.
  • Crawl the old URL list and confirm 301s land on 200 pages.
  • Check canonicals, hreflang and structured data on key templates.
  • Submit the new sitemap in Search Console.
  • Confirm analytics and conversion tracking fire correctly.

Changing domains

A domain change adds extra steps. Verify both the old and new domains in Search Console, then set up sitewide 301 redirects from each old URL to its new equivalent. Once redirects work, use the Change of Address tool in the old property.

Update everything that points to the old domain. That includes your Google Business Profile website link, citations, social profiles and paid ads. Contact sites that link to you most often and ask them to update their links.

Monitor after launch

Some movement in rankings and traffic is normal after a migration. Watch Search Console daily for the first few weeks for spikes in 404s, server errors and indexing issues. Compare organic landing page traffic against your benchmark.

Keep redirects in place for the long term. Google recommends keeping them for at least a year, and many sites keep them permanently. Review the redirect map again whenever you find old URLs still getting traffic or links.

  • Check the Pages report for new not found or excluded URLs.
  • Review crawl stats for errors.
  • Track rankings for priority keywords weekly.
  • Fix broken internal links found by a fresh crawl.

Common migration mistakes

Many traffic drops after a redesign come from a short list of avoidable errors. Teams often focus on design and forget that content, URLs and tags carry years of search history. A quick review against this list before launch catches most of them.

Content cuts are easy to miss. A cleaner design that removes text, FAQs or internal links can weaken pages that ranked well. Compare the old and new versions of your top pages side by side before you go live.

  • Leaving noindex or robots.txt blocks from staging in place.
  • Redirecting old pages to the home page in bulk.
  • Using 302 redirects where 301s are needed.
  • Dropping content or internal links from top pages.
  • Forgetting to move analytics and conversion tracking.
