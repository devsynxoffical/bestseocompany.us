How to Get More Google Reviews Without Breaking the Rules

In short

To get more Google reviews, ask every customer at the moment they are happiest, make it easy with a direct review link, and follow up once. Do not offer incentives, filter out unhappy customers before asking, or post fake reviews. Google prohibits these practices, and the FTC rule on fake reviews took effect in October 2024. Respond to every review.

Key takeaways
  • Ask every customer, because selectively asking only happy ones is review gating and is against Google's policies.
  • Google does not allow incentives for reviews, even discounts or entries into a prize draw.
  • The FTC's rule banning fake reviews and related practices took effect in October 2024.
  • Healthcare providers should never confirm someone is a patient when responding to reviews.

Why reviews matter for local businesses

Reviews are one of the clearest signals of trust for both customers and Google. Google lists prominence, which includes review count and rating, among the factors for local ranking. Customers also read reviews to decide which business to call.

Volume, recency and detail all matter. A steady flow of reviews looks more natural than a sudden burst. Detailed reviews that mention a service and a town also give customers and search engines more context.

The rules you must follow

Google's policies prohibit fake engagement, including reviews written by the business, its staff or paid third parties. They also prohibit offering anything of value in exchange for reviews and discouraging or selectively soliciting reviews. Breaking these rules can lead to removed reviews, profile warnings or restrictions.

The Federal Trade Commission's final rule on consumer reviews and testimonials took effect in October 2024. It bans fake reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews and using threats to suppress negative reviews. The FTC can seek civil penalties for knowing violations.

Ask at the right moment

Timing has a big effect on whether people follow through. The best moment is right after a visible win, such as a finished job, a solved problem or a thank-you from the customer. Ask in person first, then send the link while the experience is fresh.

Make it personal and simple. Use the customer's name, mention the job and include a one-tap link to your review form. One polite reminder a few days later is fine, but repeated nagging is not.

  • Get your direct review link from your Google Business Profile.
  • Turn it into a QR code for invoices, receipts and counter cards.
  • Train staff to ask at the end of every job or visit.
  • Automate a text or email through your CRM or booking software.
  • Track requests and reviews by team member to build the habit.

Respond to every review

Replies show customers that you care and that real people run the business. Thank positive reviewers and mention the service where it fits naturally. Keep replies short and avoid copying the same text every time.

Negative reviews deserve a calm, professional response. Acknowledge the concern, avoid arguing in public and invite the customer to continue the conversation offline. If a review breaks Google's policies, such as spam or a conflict of interest, report it through your profile, but do not expect removal just because it is negative.

Extra care for healthcare providers

Doctors, dentists, therapists and other covered entities must protect patient information under HIPAA. A review response that confirms someone is a patient, or mentions their treatment, can disclose protected health information even if the patient shared it first. Keep responses general.

Ask for reviews in a way that respects privacy as well. Do not include treatment details in review request messages. Check with your compliance advisor about your process.

  • Never confirm or deny that the reviewer is a patient.
  • Do not mention appointments, procedures or conditions.
  • Use a general statement and invite them to call the office.

Build a simple review system

Most businesses do not lack happy customers. They lack a consistent habit of asking. A simple system turns review requests into part of the normal job instead of something staff remember now and then.

Decide who asks, when they ask and how the link is sent. Write a short script for in-person requests and a template for texts and emails. Review the numbers each month and thank the team members who ask most often.

  • Add a review request step to your job completion checklist.
  • Send the link within a day of the visit.
  • Send one reminder only, a few days later.
  • Share new reviews with the team to keep the habit going.
