Local Citations and NAP Consistency: What Still Matters

In short

Local citations are online mentions of your business name, address and phone number. Their value today comes from accuracy on the platforms that matter. Claim and correct your Google, Apple, Bing and Yelp listings, submit to the main data aggregators, add key industry directories, and merge or remove duplicates so every source shows the same details.

Key takeaways
  • Accuracy on major platforms matters more than the total number of citations.
  • Data aggregators feed business data to many apps, maps and directories.
  • Apple Business Connect and Bing Places reach customers that Google does not.
  • Duplicate listings split reviews and confuse customers, so clean them up early.

What citations do today

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number, often called NAP. Years ago, building hundreds of directory listings was a common local SEO tactic. Today, more listings rarely move rankings on their own.

Citations still matter for accuracy and trust. Google, Apple, AI assistants and navigation apps pull business data from many sources. When those sources agree, your details are more likely to appear correctly wherever customers look.

Which platforms matter most

Start with the platforms customers actually use and the sources that feed other services. Then add a small number of industry and local directories that are relevant to your field. Skip low-quality directories that exist only to sell listings.

Apple Business Connect lets you manage how your business appears in Apple Maps, Siri and other Apple apps. Bing Places powers Bing search and maps and can import data from your Google Business Profile. Both take little time to set up.

Keep NAP consistent

Choose one official version of your name, address and phone number and use it everywhere. Minor formatting differences such as "St." versus "Street" are usually fine. Different phone numbers, old addresses or outdated business names cause real problems.

Watch out for tracking numbers. If you use call tracking on your website, keep your main local number as the primary number on listings and add the tracking number as an additional phone where the platform allows it. This keeps your data consistent while still measuring calls.

  • Use the same business name as your signage and Google profile.
  • Format the address the way the USPS recognizes it.
  • Use one primary local phone number.
  • Keep your website URL consistent, including https and www.

Find and clean up duplicates

Duplicate listings appear after moves, rebrands, phone changes or when someone creates a listing by mistake. They split reviews, show old information and can confuse search engines. Search each major platform for your business name, phone number and old addresses to find them.

On Google, request a merge or mark a duplicate as closed or nonexistent through the profile tools or support. On other platforms, claim the duplicate and request removal or a merge. Fix the data aggregators too, or old details may come back.

  • Search by old phone numbers and previous addresses.
  • Check for listings under former business names.
  • Look for separate listings created for individual practitioners.
  • Document each fix so you can recheck it later.

A simple citation audit process

Start by writing down your official business details in one document. Include the name, address, phone, website, hours and categories. Everyone who touches your listings should use this as the single source of truth.

Next, check each core platform, aggregator and industry directory against that document. Record the listing URL, whether you have claimed it and any errors you find. Fix the most visible platforms first, then work down the list.

Repeat the audit after any move, phone change or rebrand. These events cause most citation problems, and old details can linger for months. A short checklist makes the next audit much faster.

  • Create a master record of your business details.
  • List every listing you find in a spreadsheet.
  • Claim unclaimed listings where you can.
  • Correct errors, starting with Google, Apple, Bing and Yelp.
  • Set a reminder to recheck twice a year.

Should you use a listings service?

Paid listing management tools can push updates to many directories at once. They save time for multi-location brands and businesses that change details often. Some remove your data or revert changes if you cancel, so read the terms.

For a single-location business, manual updates on the core platforms and aggregators are often enough. Recheck the important listings twice a year and whenever your details change. A small amount of regular upkeep prevents most problems.
