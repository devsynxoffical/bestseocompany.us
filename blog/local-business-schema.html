Schema Markup for Local Businesses: A Practical Guide

In short

Local business schema is structured data, usually in JSON-LD, that describes your business to search engines. Use the most specific LocalBusiness subtype, include name and address, then add telephone, url, opening hours, geo coordinates, sameAs links and areaServed for service-area businesses. Make sure it matches visible page content, then validate it with Google's Rich Results Test.

Key takeaways
  • Use the most specific schema.org subtype, such as Plumber or Dentist, instead of the generic LocalBusiness type.
  • Google requires name and address and recommends telephone, url, geo, opening hours and priceRange.
  • Use sameAs to connect your website to your official profiles on other platforms.
  • Always validate the markup and keep it in sync with what visitors see on the page.

What LocalBusiness schema does

Schema markup is code that labels facts on your page so machines can read them. LocalBusiness schema describes your business name, address, phone, hours and more. It helps Google, Bing and AI tools connect your website with your business entity.

Schema does not guarantee a rich result or a ranking boost. It removes ambiguity and supports eligibility for some search features. Google recommends JSON-LD, which sits in a script tag and does not change how the page looks.

Choose the right subtype

Schema.org has many subtypes of LocalBusiness. Using the most specific one gives a clearer signal about what you do. If no subtype fits exactly, use the closest parent type.

Some subtypes sit under more than one branch. Dentist, for example, is both a LocalBusiness and a MedicalOrganization type. Check schema.org for the full list before you choose.

Required and recommended properties

Google's local business documentation lists name and address as required. Recommended properties include telephone, url, geo, openingHoursSpecification, priceRange and aggregateRating where appropriate. Fill in as many as you can support with accurate, visible information.

Keep in mind that Google does not show review stars for a business based on reviews it publishes about itself. You can still mark up genuine reviews on pages where they appear. Just do not expect star snippets from self-serving reviews on your own site.

  • name: your real business name.
  • address: a PostalAddress with street, city, state and ZIP.
  • telephone: your main local number.
  • url: the page for this location.
  • geo: latitude and longitude of the location.
  • openingHoursSpecification: days and hours you are open.
  • image and logo: real photos and your logo.

sameAs, hours and areaServed

The sameAs property lists URLs of your official profiles elsewhere, such as Facebook, LinkedIn, Yelp and your Google Business Profile. It helps search engines confirm that these profiles belong to the same entity. Only include profiles you control.

Use openingHoursSpecification for structured hours, including separate entries for different days. Service-area businesses that do not serve customers at their address can use areaServed to list the cities, counties or regions they cover. Keep the area consistent with your Google Business Profile.

A short JSON-LD example

Here is a minimal example for a plumber. Replace every value with your own details. Place the code in a script tag with type application/ld+json. {"@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "Plumber", "name": "Example Plumbing Co.", "url": "https://www.example.com/", "telephone": "+1-555-555-0100", "address": {"@type": "PostalAddress", "streetAddress": "123 Main St", "addressLocality": "Austin", "addressRegion": "TX", "postalCode": "78701", "addressCountry": "US"}, "openingHoursSpecification": [{"@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification", "dayOfWeek": ["Monday", "Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"], "opens": "08:00", "closes": "17:00"}], "areaServed": ["Austin", "Round Rock", "Cedar Park"], "sameAs": ["https://www.facebook.com/exampleplumbing"]}

For multiple locations, give each location page its own block with that location's details. Link each one to the main brand with the parentOrganization property. Do not put every location's address on every page.

Validate and maintain

Test every template with Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator at validator.schema.org. Fix errors first, then review warnings to see which ones are worth addressing. Recheck after site updates, since theme or plugin changes can break markup.

Make sure structured data matches the visible page. If your hours change on the page and your Google profile, update the schema too. Markup that contradicts the page can be ignored or treated as spam.

  • Run the Rich Results Test on each template.
  • Check the Enhancements reports in Search Console.
  • Update schema whenever hours, phone or address change.
  • Avoid marking up content that visitors cannot see.

Common schema mistakes to avoid

Most schema problems come from templates and plugins that add markup automatically. Two plugins can output conflicting blocks for the same business. Old hours or phone numbers can also sit in markup long after the page was updated.

Another common issue is using the wrong type or the wrong page. A generic Organization block on every page is fine, but LocalBusiness details belong on the page that describes that location. Review your markup in the page source to see what is actually being output.

  • Duplicate or conflicting LocalBusiness blocks from several plugins.
  • Hours or phone numbers that differ from the visible page.
  • Marking up reviews that are not shown on the page.
  • Using a vague type when a specific subtype exists.
  • Listing every location's address on a single page.
