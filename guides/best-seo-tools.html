What is the best SEO tool?

Short answer

There is no single best SEO tool. Google Search Console is the most important free tool because it shows how Google sees your site. Pair it with Google Analytics 4 for conversions, a crawler like Screaming Frog for technical audits, and a paid suite such as Semrush, Ahrefs or Moz for keyword and backlink research.

Key takeaways
  • Start with free tools from Google and Bing before paying for anything.
  • Search Console is the closest thing to a must-have SEO tool.
  • Crawlers find technical issues that suites and analytics can miss.
  • Paid suites help most with keyword research and competitor analysis.

Pick tools by job, not by brand

SEO covers several different jobs, and no single tool does all of them well. Some tools show your own site's data from the search engine. Others crawl your site like a bot, and others estimate data about the wider web and your competitors.

The right setup depends on what you need to answer. A local business may only need Search Console, Analytics and its Google Business Profile. An agency or an ecommerce team will likely add a crawler and a paid research suite.

Start with the free tools. They use real data from search engines, which estimates from third-party tools cannot fully replace. Add paid tools when you have a clear question the free ones cannot answer.

Free tools from search engines

Google Search Console shows which searches bring impressions and clicks, which pages are indexed, and what problems Google found. It also lets you inspect individual URLs and submit sitemaps. If you only use one SEO tool, use this one.

Google Analytics 4 tracks what visitors do after they arrive. Use it to see which organic landing pages lead to calls, form fills or purchases. Bing Webmaster Tools offers similar site data for Bing, which also matters because some AI assistants draw on Bing's index.

PageSpeed Insights tests a page's loading performance and Core Web Vitals, using lab data and real-user field data where available. Google Business Profile manages how a local business appears in Maps and local results, including reviews, hours and photos.

  • Google Search Console: search performance, indexing and crawl issues.
  • Google Analytics 4: traffic behavior and conversions from organic search.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools: Bing performance, indexing and site scans.
  • PageSpeed Insights: page speed and Core Web Vitals checks.
  • Google Business Profile: local listing, reviews and Maps visibility.

Crawlers and technical tools

Screaming Frog SEO Spider crawls your site the way a search engine bot would. It finds broken links, redirect chains, missing or duplicate titles, thin pages, canonical issues and much more. It is a desktop program with a free version that has a crawl limit and a paid license for larger sites.

Schema validators check your structured data. Google's Rich Results Test shows whether a page qualifies for rich results in Google, and the Schema Markup Validator at schema.org checks general schema syntax. Use both whenever you add or change markup.

A crawler is often where real technical problems show up first. Analytics shows a traffic drop, but a crawl tells you why, such as a template that started adding noindex tags or a migration that broke redirects.

Paid all-in-one SEO suites

Semrush, Ahrefs and Moz are the best-known paid SEO suites. Each builds its own index of keywords and links, then estimates search volume, keyword difficulty, competitor rankings and backlinks. Their numbers are estimates, so use them to compare and prioritize rather than as exact counts.

Semrush is known for a broad toolkit that covers keyword research, competitor analysis, site audits, rank tracking and marketing features beyond SEO. Ahrefs is known for its backlink index and strong keyword and content research. Moz is known for its Domain Authority metric, keyword research and an approachable interface.

All three overlap heavily, and plans and features change often. Use free trials or limited free versions where offered, and pick the one whose data and workflow your team actually uses.

A simple tool stack for most businesses

Most small and mid-sized businesses can do a lot with a lean stack. Set up Search Console, Analytics 4 and Bing Webmaster Tools on day one. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile if you serve a local area.

For a quick health check, run your site through our free SEO audit tool at /tools/seo-audit-tool. It flags common technical and on-page issues in your browser. For a deeper review, request our free 40-point audit and a strategist will look at your site by hand.

Add a crawler and a paid suite when you start publishing content regularly or competing on harder keywords. Tools only help if someone reviews the data and acts on it, so budget time for analysis as well as subscriptions.

Tools for AI search visibility

AI answer engines such as Google's AI features, ChatGPT and Perplexity are a newer place where people find businesses. Tooling for this area is still young, and many products only sample a set of prompts and report where a brand appears. Treat those reports as directional.

The basics still come from the tools above. Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools show whether your pages are indexed and discoverable. Schema validators confirm your structured data is correct, and a crawler checks that important content is in the HTML and not hidden behind scripts.

A practical habit is to ask AI assistants the questions your customers ask and note which sources they cite. Compare those sources with your own pages to see what is missing, such as a clear answer, a comparison or up-to-date business details.
