How can SEO be improved?

Short answer

To improve SEO, fix technical issues that block crawling and indexing, match each page to one search intent, refresh content that is outdated, add internal links between related pages, show real expertise and trust, earn relevant links, strengthen local signals, and measure results in Search Console and Analytics so you can repeat what works.

Key takeaways
  • Fix crawling and indexing problems before anything else.
  • Refreshing existing pages is often faster than publishing new ones.
  • Internal links and clear titles are high-impact, low-effort wins.
  • Measure leads and conversions, not just rankings.

Start with technical fixes

Search engines need to find, crawl and index your pages before they can rank. Technical issues can quietly hold back a whole site. Check these first because every other improvement depends on them.

Open Search Console and look at the page indexing report. Note important pages that are excluded and find out why. Then run a crawl with a tool like Screaming Frog or our free audit tool to catch broken links and redirect problems.

  • Confirm important pages are indexed and not blocked by robots.txt or noindex.
  • Fix broken internal links and remove redirect chains.
  • Set correct canonical tags to handle duplicate pages.
  • Submit an up-to-date XML sitemap in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • Make sure the site works well on mobile and uses HTTPS everywhere.

Improve on-page SEO

Each page should target one main topic and the search intent behind it. Look at what already ranks for your target search. If the top results are guides, a sales page will struggle, and if they are product pages, a long article may not fit.

Write a unique title tag that includes the main phrase and gives a reason to click. Use one clear H1, logical subheadings and a meta description that summarizes the page. Add descriptive alt text to meaningful images.

Put the most useful information near the top. Answer the main question early, then add detail. This helps readers, and it makes your page easier for search features and AI answers to quote.

Refresh content and strengthen internal links

Many sites have pages that once ranked well and slowly slipped. Search Console shows these as pages with falling clicks or impressions. Updating them is often faster than writing something new, because they already have some history and links.

When you refresh a page, update facts and examples, cover questions people now ask, remove outdated sections and improve the structure. Change the date only when the content has really changed. Merge thin pages that cover the same topic into one stronger page and redirect the old URLs.

Internal links tell search engines which pages matter and how topics connect. Link from strong, relevant pages to the ones you want to rank. Use descriptive anchor text and make sure no important page is left without internal links.

Show experience, expertise and trust

Google's quality rater guidelines describe experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust, often shortened to E-E-A-T. These are not a single ranking factor. They describe what helpful, reliable content looks like, and the signals that support them help both people and search engines.

Show who is behind your content and why they know the subject. Use real examples, original photos and specific steps from your own work. Keep contact details, policies and business information easy to find.

  • Add author bios with relevant experience and credentials.
  • Include original photos, examples and process details.
  • Cite reputable sources when you reference facts.
  • Show reviews, licenses, certifications and a real address where relevant.
  • Keep an About page and contact page that are complete and current.

Earn links, build local signals and improve page experience

Links from relevant, trusted sites still help pages rank. Focus on earning them with useful resources, original data, partnerships, local sponsorships and press coverage. Avoid buying links in bulk, which can lead to penalties and wasted budget.

If you serve customers in a specific area, complete your Google Business Profile with accurate categories, services, hours and photos. Ask happy customers for reviews and reply to them. Keep your business name, address and phone number consistent across directories and your own site.

Page experience matters for users and supports SEO. Check key templates in PageSpeed Insights and the Core Web Vitals report. Compress images, limit heavy scripts, avoid layout shifts and remove intrusive pop-ups on mobile.

Make content easy for AI answers to use

AI answer engines summarize pages and often cite their sources. Pages with clear structure and direct answers are easier for them to quote. The same habits also help with featured snippets in regular search.

Start key sections with a short, plain answer to the question in the heading. Use headings that match how people phrase questions. Add relevant schema markup, such as Organization, LocalBusiness, Product or FAQ where it fits, and validate it.

Keep your business name, services, locations and contact details consistent on your site, your Google Business Profile and major directories. Inconsistent details make it harder for any system to trust which version is correct.

Prioritize by impact and effort

You cannot do everything at once. Rank tasks by how much they are likely to help and how much work they take. Start with high-impact, low-effort tasks, then plan the larger projects.

Impact varies by site. A site with indexing problems gains the most from technical fixes, while a technically clean site may gain more from content and links. Use the table as a general guide and adjust it to your audit findings.

Measure what matters

Track progress so you know which changes work. In Search Console, watch impressions, clicks and average position for priority pages. In Analytics 4, track organic conversions such as calls, form fills and purchases.

Keep a simple log of changes with dates. When rankings or traffic move, you can match the change to what you did. Review results monthly and plan the next set of tasks each quarter.
